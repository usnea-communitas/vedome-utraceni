# Mindful Spending: a 90-minute lesson

A 50 / 30 / 20 money plan, practised on a made-up story. For groups aged 15 and over, ideally 8–30 people. Participants work in the Mindful Spending app (Vědomé utrácení) on their own phone, or in pairs on one, and on paper worksheets. A shorter version is in [lesson-45-minutes.md](lesson-45-minutes.md).

Amounts are in Czech crowns (Kč), as in the app. The stories are set in Czechia; you can adapt them to your group in words.

## What participants take away

After the lesson a participant:

1. splits a monthly income into Need, Fun and Future me, and knows why Future me gets paid at the start of the month;
2. tells fixed, controllable and one-off expenses apart and finds a "leak" among small expenses;
3. sets a goal with an amount and a date and works out how many months it takes;
4. decides where an unexpected expense is paid from and knows what an emergency fund is for;
5. weighs up when borrowing makes sense and when it does not;
6. takes one idea into their own plan, privately; nobody has to show it to anyone.

Which points of the Czech Financial Literacy Standard (Ministry of Finance, 2017) the lesson covers is in the [table](financial-literacy-standard.md).

## Before you start

- **Try the workshop yourself**: open the link, pick a story and go through the five tasks (about 15 minutes).
- **Phones**: one per participant, or one per pair. Nothing is installed and nobody signs in. The first load needs the internet; after that the workshop also works offline.
- **A projector** with the [slides](slides.md); slide 6 has the QR code.
- **Print** [worksheets](worksheets.md) 1–3 for everyone (or every pair), and worksheet 4 and the [questionnaire](questionnaire.md) for everyone. Pens; the phone's calculator is enough.
- The workshop link: **vedome-utraceni.vercel.app/?workshop=1&lang=en** (Czech `lang=cs`; also `pl`, `uk`, `es`).

## Ground rules

- **We work with made-up stories.** Nobody has to talk about their own money. Anyone who wants to try their own numbers does so alone, on worksheet 4.
- **Fun is part of the plan.** We do not judge what anyone spends on; we look at what makes sense to whom.
- **The data stay on the phone.** The workshop keeps its own data, apart from the participant's own, and one button deletes them at the end.
- The amounts in the stories are **net income**: what arrives in the account. For a salary, that is the gross pay minus tax and social and health insurance.

### 0–10 Introduction

- Ask: "Do you know the feeling at the end of the month of not knowing where the money went?" A raised hand is enough; nobody explains anything.
- The **50 / 30 / 20** rule: half of the income for **Need** (housing, food, transport, phone: what you cannot do without), 30 % for **Fun** (what makes life pleasant: coffee, cinema, subscriptions, gifts), 20 % for **Future me** (an emergency fund, saving for a goal, later also investing).
- It is a starting frame, not a law. When, for example, rent takes more than half of the income, the ratios can be changed in the app (More → Category ratios).
- **Future me first:** whoever pays their future self right after payday does not rely on something being left over.
- Say that the amounts in the stories are net income (see Ground rules), and that the data stay on the phone and are deleted at the end.

### 10–15 Choosing a story

- Show the QR code (slide 6). Everyone picks one of three made-up stories:
  - **Ema, 19** – a student with a part-time job, lives with her parents, income 11,600 Kč;
  - **Tomáš, 20** – first paycheck, a rented room, income 27,500 Kč;
  - **Lucie, 24** – moving in together with her boyfriend, income 32,000 Kč.
- Make sure all three stories are in the room; it helps when comparing. In pairs: one phone, two heads.

### 15–30 Task 1: Split the income

- In the app: the **Workshop** tab → task 1 → **Open Overview** → split the income in one tap. Then in **Plan**, break at least one part into real items.
- On **worksheet 1** they write the split in crowns and, for the items already spent, the part of the plan and the kind of expense:
  - **fixed** – the same every month, cannot be changed in the short term (rent, phone, travel pass);
  - **controllable** – you decide how much as you go (coffee, food delivery, shopping);
  - **one-off** – irregular, often unexpected (a repair, a new fridge).
- To check:

| Story | Income | Need 50 % | Fun 30 % | Future me 20 % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ema | 11,600 Kč | 5,800 Kč | 3,480 Kč | 2,320 Kč |
| Tomáš | 27,500 Kč | 13,750 Kč | 8,250 Kč | 5,500 Kč |
| Lucie | 32,000 Kč | 16,000 Kč | 9,600 Kč | 6,400 Kč |

- Ema lives with her parents: so far she has spent 699 Kč of Need. It is worth asking whether different ratios, for example more for Future me, would make sense for her.
- Questions: What belongs in Need and what already in Fun (a phone yes, but the most expensive plan)? Why pay Future me at the start of the month rather than from what is left? Is Ema's café job a regular income? What if it ended in the summer?

### 30–45 Task 2: Find the leak

- In the app: **Overview** shows what the money has gone on this month. Which small thing added up the most?
- **Worksheet 2**: the monthly amount × 12 = the cost per year; what else the yearly amount could buy; what could replace the small thing so that the fun stays. Then plan a smaller amount in **Plan**.
- A "leak" is not a bad expense. It is one nobody decided on consciously.
- To check:

| Story | Small thing | Per month | Per year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ema | coffee on the way to class (12 × 65 Kč) | 780 Kč | 9,360 Kč |
| Ema | three streaming services | 597 Kč | 7,164 Kč |
| Tomáš | food delivery (6 × 290 Kč) | 1,740 Kč | 20,880 Kč |
| Tomáš | little online buys | 1,150 Kč | 13,800 Kč |
| Lucie | dinners out (4 × 450 Kč) | 1,800 Kč | 21,600 Kč |
| Lucie | music, video and cloud subscriptions | 657 Kč | 7,884 Kč |

- Questions: What would the small thing cost in a year? What could replace it so that the fun stays? Which subscription would be missed least?

### 45–55 Task 3: Set a goal

- In the app: **Goals** → add the story's goal: Ema a driving licence, Tomáš a three-month cushion, Lucie a holiday together.
- The story does not set the target amount; the pair or the group estimates it. Tomáš works his out: three months of necessary expenses.
- **Worksheet 3**: target amount ÷ monthly for Future me = number of months (round up). Monthly for Future me: Ema 2,320 Kč, Tomáš 5,500 Kč, Lucie 6,400 Kč.
- Example: Tomáš, 3 × 13,750 Kč = 41,250 Kč; 41,250 ÷ 5,500 = 7.5, so **8 months**.
- Questions: What happens when a goal has no amount and no date? What could hold the person up on the way, and how can it be prevented?

### 55–65 Task 4: An unexpected expense

- Announce it to everyone at once: "Something unexpected has happened." Each story has its own surprise: **Ema** a cracked phone screen, 2,490 Kč; **Tomáš** a bike repair after a fall, 1,800 Kč; **Lucie** half a new fridge, 4,500 Kč.
- In the app: task 4 → **Add expense** (it is filled in). Which part of the plan will pay for it, and what will then be short of money? They write it on **worksheet 3**.
- To check what is left of Need: Ema 5,800 − 699 − 2,490 = **2,611 Kč**; Tomáš 13,750 − 11,900 − 1,800 = **50 Kč**; Lucie 16,000 − 11,700 − 4,500 = **−200 Kč**: she is 200 Kč short and has to take it from Fun or from her emergency fund.
- An **emergency fund** is money set aside for exactly such moments. A common recommendation is to keep several months of necessary expenses in it (three to six).
- Questions: Which part did you pay it from, and what was then short of money? How big a fund would cover a surprise like this?

### 65–75 When the plan does not add up

- A budget is **balanced** when expenses equal income, in **surplus** when something is left over, and in **deficit** when expenses are higher than income. There are four ways out of a deficit: spend less, earn more (a part-time job, selling things you do not need), postpone the expense, or borrow.
- In pairs they decide on four situations (slide 13): borrow – yes, no, or what does it depend on?
  1. A new phone in instalments, because the old one is slow.
  2. Repairing a fridge you cannot cook without, when the emergency fund is not enough.
  3. A holiday on a credit card.
  4. A loan to pay off another loan you cannot keep up with.
- Criteria to discuss: Is it a need or a want? Can I make the repayments from the plan, even in a worse month? How much extra will I pay? Offers are compared by the **APRC** (in Czech RPSN), which includes the interest and the fees. What if my income drops?
- Notes for you: (1) rather not: it is Fun – postpone it and save up; (2) can make sense when the expense is necessary, the loan as cheap as possible and the repayments fit the plan; (3) spending on Fun with borrowed money is risky; (4) a warning sign: debts are starting to pile up and it is time to get help.
- If someone starts talking about their own debts, offer to talk after the lesson and point to free debt advice. In Czechia, for example, Poradna při finanční tísni (financnitisen.cz, free helpline 800 722 722).

### 75–83 Task 5: Looking back and my own plan

- In the app: **Reflection** → answer the questions as the person in the story. In pairs: What could be done differently next month? What will you take into your own plan? Then two or three thoughts aloud for the whole group.
- Everyone fills in **worksheet 4** for themselves and keeps it. Nothing is handed in.

### 83–87 The end

- Everyone taps **End and delete** (at the bottom of the Workshop tab or in More). On shared devices, always. The participants' own data in that browser stay as they were.
- Anyone who wants to use the app for real opens **vedome-utraceni.vercel.app** without the workshop link. Their data stay only in their browser.

### 87–90 Questionnaire

- Hand out the [questionnaire](questionnaire.md). It is anonymous and nobody signs it; collect it in an envelope or a box.

## Optional: Future me and compound interest (5 minutes)

When the group asks about investing, show the **Tips** tab on the projector from your own app, with its compound-interest calculator: a monthly deposit, a number of years and a return of 6, 8 or 10 % a year; the chart separates the money put in from the gains. In the workshop the Workshop tab takes the place of Tips, so show it from your own app. Stress that the return is not guaranteed, investing carries risk, and the calculator is an illustration, not a recommendation.

## Appendix: the stories on paper

The same details as in the app (made up, amounts illustrative). Useful when phones do not work or there are too few of them.

**Ema, 19 – a student with a part-time job.** She is in her first year at university and works in a café at weekends. She lives with her parents, so she pays neither rent nor food at home, but she does pay for her phone, travel and fun.

| Income | Kč | Spent this month | Kč | Part |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Café job | 9,600 | Phone | 399 | Need |
| Pocket money from parents | 2,000 | Monthly travel pass | 300 | Need |
| | | Coffee on the way to class (12×) | 780 | Fun |
| | | Three streaming services | 597 | Fun |

Goal: a driving licence. Unexpected expense: a cracked phone screen, 2,490 Kč.

**Tomáš, 20 – first paycheck.** After finishing school he started his first job. He rents a room and is paying for everything himself for the first time.

| Income | Kč | Spent this month | Kč | Part |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pay | 27,500 | Room rent | 9,500 | Need |
| | | Food shopping | 2,400 | Need |
| | | Food delivery (6×) | 1,740 | Fun |
| | | Little online buys | 1,150 | Fun |

Goal: a three-month cushion. Unexpected expense: a bike repair after a fall, 1,800 Kč.

**Lucie, 24 – moving in together.** She and her boyfriend have rented their first flat. They split rent, utilities and shopping in half; each keeps their own money.

| Income | Kč | Spent this month | Kč | Part |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pay | 32,000 | Half the rent | 8,500 | Need |
| | | Shared shopping (her share) | 3,200 | Need |
| | | Dinners out (4×) | 1,800 | Fun |
| | | Music, video and cloud subscriptions | 657 | Fun |

Goal: a holiday together. Unexpected expense: half a new fridge, 4,500 Kč.
